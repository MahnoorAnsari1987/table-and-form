Sacred & historic landscapes

Islamic Places

From the Two Holy Mosques to cities of learning and stone

A geography of meaning

Some sites are sacred in worship. Others are historically important as centers of learning, power, or architectural genius. A well-known hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim states that journey should not be undertaken to visit mosques except three: Masjid al-Haram, the Prophet's Mosque, and Masjid al-Aqsa. Other locations below are presented for historical and cultural education.

Makkah, Arabian Peninsula

Makkah & Masjid al-Haram

The holiest city in Islam. The Kaaba is the qiblah. Hajj, one of the Five Pillars, centers on rites in and around Makkah described in the Qur'an (for example, 2:196-200 and 22:27-29).

  • Historical importance: prophetic birthplace and site of the Kaaba
  • Living practice: Hajj and Umrah
Madinah

Madinah & Al-Masjid an-Nabawi

The city that received the Prophet ﷺ after the Hijrah. The mosque he established became the center of the first Muslim community. Visiting Madinah is an act of love and greeting, distinct from Hajj itself.

  • Historical importance: capital of the early ummah
  • Includes Quba and other early mosques in the city's memory
Jerusalem

Masjid al-Aqsa

The noble sanctuary (al-Haram al-Sharif) includes Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock. The Qur'an refers to the "farthest mosque" in the Night Journey (Qur'an 17:1), a verse classically linked to this precinct.

  • Third of the three mosques of special merit in hadith
  • A layered history of Umayyad architecture
Egypt

Historic Cairo

A UNESCO-recognized urban landscape of Fatimid, Ayyubid, and Mamluk mosques, madrasas, and markets. Al-Azhar, founded in the 10th century, remains a major institution of Sunni learning.

  • Ibn Tulun Mosque is among the oldest surviving congregational mosques
  • A living city of scholarship and craft
Al-Andalus

The Mosque-Cathedral of Cordoba

Begun in the 8th century under Abd al-Rahman I, the hypostyle hall of repeating horseshoe arches is one of the most recognizable interiors in Islamic architectural history.

  • Symbol of Umayyad Spain
  • Later Christian additions show layered heritage
Anatolia

Istanbul's imperial mosques

Ottoman architects, especially Mimar Sinan, shaped a skyline of cascading domes. The Sultan Ahmed Mosque (Blue Mosque) faces Hagia Sophia across a historic square.

  • A bridge between Byzantine and Islamic forms
  • Still a city of active congregational worship
Central Asia

Samarkand & the Registan

A Timurid ensemble of madrasas famous for turquoise tile, iwans, and geometric ornament - a Silk Road crossroads of Persianate Islamic culture.

  • Centers of astronomy and manuscript culture nearby
  • Outstanding example of iwan-madrasa planning
Maghrib

Fez and al-Qarawiyyin

Al-Qarawiyyin, founded in the 9th century by Fatima al-Fihri according to historical tradition, is often cited among the world's oldest continually operating institutions of learning.

  • A heart of Maghribi scholarship
  • Zellij, wood carving, and courtyard mosques
Ifriqiya

The Great Mosque of Kairouan

Founded in the 7th century and rebuilt in later Aghlabid phases, it is a landmark of early Maghribi mosque design and a historic pilgrimage city of North Africa.

  • Influential hypostyle plan and massive minaret
  • A foundation city of Islamic North Africa

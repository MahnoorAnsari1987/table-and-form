Qur'anic memory

The Prophets

Selected messengers named in the Qur'an - presented without images of their persons

An educational note

Muslims believe God sent many prophets. The Qur'an names twenty-five. This page introduces a selection with short, source-aware summaries. We do not depict the Prophets visually. Peace be upon them all.

حضرت آدم علیہ السلام

Adam (peace be upon him)

The first human and prophet in Islamic belief. The Qur'an recounts his creation, a test in the garden, and turning back to God in repentance (see Qur'an 2:30-37 and 7:19-25).

  • Lesson: human dignity and accountability
  • Lesson: repentance is always open
حضرت نوح علیہ السلام

Nuh (peace be upon him)

A messenger who called his people for a long time. Surah Nuh (Qur'an 71) and other passages describe steadfast invitation, rejection by many, and deliverance of the believers.

  • Lesson: patience in conveying truth
  • Lesson: results belong to God
حضرت ابراہیم علیہ السلام

Ibrahim (peace be upon him)

Called Khalil Allah, a model of tawhid (oneness of God). The Qur'an links him and Ismail with raising the foundations of the House in Makkah (Qur'an 2:125-127).

  • Lesson: sincere devotion over custom
  • Lesson: hospitality and trust in God
حضرت موسیٰ علیہ السلام

Musa (peace be upon him)

The Qur'an narrates his confrontation with Pharaoh, the Exodus, and receiving scripture. His story is among the most detailed in the Qur'an (for example, Surah Ta-Ha and Al-Qasas).

  • Lesson: standing against oppression
  • Lesson: relying on God in fear
حضرت عیسیٰ علیہ السلام

Isa (peace be upon him)

Born to Maryam (peace be upon her). The Qur'an affirms his prophethood and miraculous birth (Qur'an 19; 3:45-47) and describes him as a messenger, not divine.

  • Lesson: humility and compassion
  • Lesson: honoring Maryam's station
حضرت محمد ﷺ

Muhammad ﷺ

The final prophet in Islamic belief (Qur'an 33:40 describes him as the Seal of the Prophets). The Qur'an was revealed to him; his life is studied through the Qur'an, hadith, and sira - never through portraits on this site.

  • Lesson: mercy to the worlds (Qur'an 21:107)
  • Lesson: character as a living example
حضرت یوسف علیہ السلام

Yusuf (peace be upon him)

Surah Yusuf (Qur'an 12) is a continuous narrative of trial, integrity, and eventual reunion. It is often studied for its literary and ethical depth.

  • Lesson: integrity under pressure
  • Lesson: forgiveness after harm
حضرت داؤد علیہ السلام

Dawud (peace be upon him)

A prophet-king in the Qur'anic account, given the Zabur. The Qur'an mentions his justice and devotion (for example, Qur'an 38:17-26).

  • Lesson: power paired with justice
  • Lesson: remembrance of God
حضرت سلیمان علیہ السلام

Sulayman (peace be upon him)

Known for wisdom and a kingdom described in the Qur'an as a gift from God (see Qur'an 27 and 34). His story is told without needing visual invention.

  • Lesson: gratitude for gifts
  • Lesson: wisdom over spectacle
حضرت اسماعیل علیہ السلام

Ismail (peace be upon him)

Son of Ibrahim. The Qur'an links him with patience, the House in Makkah, and a people of prayer (Qur'an 2:125-127; 19:54-55). Sa'i between Safa and Marwah remembers Hajar's trust in God.

  • Lesson: trust during hardship
  • Lesson: a lineage of tawhid in Makkah
حضرت یونس علیہ السلام

Yunus (peace be upon him)

The companion of the fish. His prayer in darkness - "There is no god but You, glory be to You" (Qur'an 21:87) - is recited as a dua of relief. His people believed and were spared.

  • Lesson: never despair of God's mercy
  • Lesson: a whole city can turn back
هود

Hud (peace be upon him)

Sent to 'Ad. Surah Hud and al-Ahqaf warn against pride in strength and buildings. The lesson is humility before the One who gives power.

  • Lesson: power is a test
  • Lesson: prophets call to Allah alone

Arabic names appear as ornamental calligraphy, not as portraits. Additional prophets named in the Qur'an include Idris, Hud, Salih, Lut, Ismail, Ishaq, Yaqub, Ayyub, Shu'ayb, Harun, Dhul-Kifl, Yunus, Zakariyya, and Yahya (peace be upon them).

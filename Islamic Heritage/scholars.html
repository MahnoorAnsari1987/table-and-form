'Ilm - knowledge

Islamic Scholars

Selected teachers of law, hadith, philosophy, and the sciences

A tradition of learning

From the mosque circle to the madrasa, Muslim scholars developed methods for preserving the Qur'an and hadith, reasoning about law (fiqh), and studying nature and society. The names below are a small, educational sample - not a complete canon.

امام بخاری
Hadith

Imam al-Bukhari

Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (d. 870 CE) compiled Sahih al-Bukhari, among the most carefully sifted hadith collections in Sunni tradition.

  • Field: hadith criticism and compilation
  • Work: Al-Jami' al-Sahih
امام مالک
Law

Imam Malik

Malik ibn Anas (d. 795 CE) of Madinah. His Muwatta gathers practice of Madinah with hadith. The Maliki school remains influential, especially in North and West Africa.

  • Field: fiqh and hadith of Madinah
  • Work: Al-Muwatta
امام ابو حنیفہ
Law

Imam Abu Hanifa

Nu'man ibn Thabit (d. 767 CE) of Kufa. Founder-figure of the Hanafi school, known for reasoned analogical work (qiyas) alongside textual sources.

  • Field: jurisprudence
  • Legacy: the most widespread Sunni legal school today
امام شافعی
Law

Imam al-Shafi'i

Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i (d. 820 CE). His Risala is a foundational text in usul al-fiqh (legal theory), organizing how Qur'an, sunnah, consensus, and analogy are used.

  • Field: legal theory
  • Work: Al-Risala
امام ابو حامد الغزالی
Thought

Abu Hamid al-Ghazali

Al-Ghazali (d. 1111 CE) wrote across law, theology, and spiritual ethics. Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din remains widely read as a synthesis of practice and inner life.

  • Fields: Shafi'i law, kalam, ethics
  • Work: Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din
ابن سینا
Sciences

Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

Ibn Sina (d. 1037 CE) was a philosopher-physician. The Canon of Medicine was a standard reference in much of the medieval world, East and West.

  • Fields: medicine, philosophy
  • Work: Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb
الخوارزمی
Sciences

Al-Khwarizmi

Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (9th century, Baghdad). His algebra treatise helped give the discipline its name; Latin translations spread his methods in Europe. The word "algorithm" traces to his name.

  • Fields: mathematics, astronomy
  • Work: Al-Kitab al-Mukhtasar fi Hisab al-Jabr
ابن خلدون
Thought

Ibn Khaldun

Abd al-Rahman Ibn Khaldun (d. 1406 CE). The Muqaddimah analyzes social cohesion ('asabiyya), urban life, and the rise and fall of states - a landmark of historical sociology.

  • Fields: history, social thought
  • Work: Al-Muqaddimah
ابن رشد
Thought

Ibn Rushd (Averroes)

Ibn Rushd of Cordoba (d. 1198 CE) wrote commentaries on Aristotle and argued for the harmony of demonstration and revelation in works such as Fasl al-Maqal.

  • Fields: philosophy, Maliki law, medicine
  • Legacy: major influence on medieval Latin thought
فاطمة
Institutions

Fatima al-Fihri

According to Maghribi historical tradition, she founded al-Qarawiyyin in Fez in the 9th century - mosque, madrasa, and one of the world's oldest living houses of learning.

  • Field: waqf and education
  • Legacy: knowledge as a woman's endowment
ابن الهيثم
Sciences

Ibn al-Haytham

Hasan Ibn al-Haytham (d. c. 1040) transformed the study of optics and the experimental method. His Book of Optics influenced later science in East and West.

  • Fields: optics, mathematics
  • Work: Kitab al-Manazir

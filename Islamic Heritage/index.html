The three mosques of special journey

A well-known hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim limits special travel for mosque-visiting to these three precincts.

Masjid al-Haram

Makkah. The Kaaba is the qiblah. Hajj and ʿUmrah fulfill Qur’an 22:27 and the fifth pillar.

Al-Masjid an-Nabawi

Madinah. The mosque of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the Rawdah, and the first Muslim civic community.

Masjid al-Aqsa

Jerusalem’s noble sanctuary (al-Haram al-Sharif). Linked in tafsir to the Night Journey (Qur’an 17:1). The Dome of the Rock (completed 691–92 CE) stands within the same precinct.

Form & meaning

Why Islamic architecture looks this way

Because worship is toward one God, mosque interiors avoid statues. Beauty is carried by the Word, repeating geometry that hints at infinity, courtyards of water and shade, and a minaret that calls the city to salah.

Signposts of Islamic history

610 CE

First revelation

Iqraʾ in the Cave of Hira (Qur’an 96:1–5). Twenty-three years of wahy follow.

622 CE / 1 AH

Hijrah

The Islamic calendar begins. Madinah becomes the home of the ummah.

632–661

Rightly Guided Caliphs

Abu Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthman, and ʿAli (may Allah be pleased with them). The ʿUthmanic mushaf is standardized.

8th–14th c.

Age of ʿilm

Hadith canons, four madhhabs, House of Wisdom, Andalus, and the great mosques.
